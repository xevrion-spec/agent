- **The free workplace check** (`workplace_check.py`, xevrion.co.uk/check). `POST /v1/public/workplace-check`
  `{domain, team_size?, email?, consent_updates?, source?}` reads only the public website and public DNS: email
  protection (SPF, DKIM, DMARC through `domain_check.analyse`), website basics (HTTPS, certificate days left, HSTS,
  framing protection, nosniff), customer-response signals from up to four pages (`outreach_signals`, at most six quoted
  words), the coordination hours for the team size (`coordination.team_estimate`, an estimate from Asana 2021) and the
  national average expected loss (£688 a year, DSIT 2025, never a prediction). Each check is pass, attention or fail
  with one sentence and one "how Xevrion helps" line; the score is email 45 + website 30 + response 25 points (a part
  that could not be read counts half) and the top three fixes put failures first.
- **SSRF-safe reading**: the domain is normalised (scheme, path, port, user, www. removed; IDNA) and must be a public
  registrable name (no IPs, localhost, internal or reserved names, bare suffixes or xevrion.co.uk). Every hop is
  resolved and refused if any answer is private, loopback, link-local, multicast, reserved or an IPv4 inside IPv6, then
  pinned to the socket on port 80 or 443; redirects must stay on the site and may not leave HTTPS; robots.txt is
  respected; the whole check stops within 12 seconds and each page is capped at `public_web.MAX_BYTES`.
- **Abuse limits and cache**: an in-memory token bucket per network (6 at once, then one every two minutes), a daily
  cap on fresh checks (`XEVRION_WORKPLACE_CHECK_DAILY_CAP`, default 400) and a domain's findings kept for 24 hours
  (`workplace_checks`, keyed by sha256(domain)), so checking it again is free.
- **Reports by unguessable code**: 32 URL-safe characters (192 bits); only sha256(code) is stored

- **Customer engine 4a: deliverability and legal guards for owner outreach** (`outreach_guards.py`, `outreach_copy.py`).
  - **One-click unsubscribe on every outreach email** (first emails and follow-ups; RFC 8058, Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender
    rules, PECR, ICO do-not-contact): `List-Unsubscribe: <https://api.xevrion.co.uk/v1/public/unsubscribe/{token}>,
    <mailto:{sending mailbox}?subject=unsubscribe>` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, plus a
    plain footer line under the existing "reply no thanks" one: "Sent by Xevrion, a UK company. Unsubscribe: <link>".
    The token is HMAC-signed with `XEVRION_UNSUBSCRIBE_KEY` when set (independent of operator-token rotation), otherwise
    with a key derived from `EMPIRE_OPERATOR_TOKEN` under its own purpose label; verification accepts either, so links
    already sent survive setting the dedicated key. It names the tenant, the campaign and a keyed recipient hash: no
    readable address. POST
    suppresses at once and answers 200; GET only shows a "Confirm unsubscribe" page whose button POSTs, so link
    scanners unsubscribe nobody. An unsubscribe (link or the mailto reply) suppresses the address, the company domain
    for outreach (never a free-mail domain; the free check still reaches a colleague) and excludes the company from
    scheduling, all with the reason "unsubscribe". Production never sends an outreach email without a signing key.
    Gmail threading of follow-ups is unchanged. The Gmail adapter writes List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post
    only in their RFC forms and with the 998-character line limit, so the long header is never folded into an RFC 2047
    encoded word; the transactional broker's free-check List-Unsubscribe had that fault with real opt-out URLs and is
    fixed the same way. Microsoft 365 mailboxes get the footer link (Graph cannot set List-* headers).
  - **Complaint, unsubscribe and bounce guards, kept apart.** Per mailbox and day: sent, bounced, unsubscribed,
    negative replies (OPT_OUT) and spam-signal replies (spam, reported, GDPR, ICO, remove me, how did you get ...),
    judged on the person's own words. Complaints are spam-signal replies only, a **proxy** for Gmail's spam rate
    because Postmaster data is not connected (the console says so): a mailbox pauses for 48 hours over 0.3% from 200
    delivered, or on 2 spam-signal replies under 200 delivered, tightening to over 0.1% from 500 delivered; all
    outreach pauses for 48 hours over 0.3% from 500 delivered (ceiling 0.3%). Unsubscribes measure targeting, not spam:
    a mailbox pauses for 48 hours over 2% from 200 delivered (ceiling 3%). The 7-day bounce guard pauses a mailbox over
    2% from 200 sends (ceiling 2%; the same-day BOUNCE_HOLD stays). A finished pause starts the window again.
    Thresholds can be lowered (`PUT /v1/operator/outreach-guards/settings`) but never raised above their ceilings
    (stored values over one are clamped); the owner can lift a pause early (`POST /v1/operator/outreach-guards/lift`,
    audited). Every pause is logged (`outreach_guard_paused`), audited and shown in the console; the daily run's
    figures carry `guard_pauses` and `guards_7_days` (spam signals, unsubscribes, bounces and their rates).
  - **Warm-up ramp.** A mailbox's daily cap follows the days it has sent on, guard-pause days not counted: 10, 15, 20,
    25, 30, 35, 40, 45, then 50 (PER_MAILBOX_DAILY stays the maximum). Mailboxes that first sent before the ramp, 14 or
    more days ago, count as warmed. Spacing stays 4-8 minutes per mailbox.
  - **Registered companies only, at send time**: `may_email_company(prospect) -> (bool, reason)` gates every first
    email: `company_verified is True` (Companies House, step 4b) or, until then, Ltd / Limited / LLP / PLC in the
    company's own evidence, which prepared contacts now carry (`corporate_evidence`, `site_title`). Held first emails
    are recorded once (`outreach_company_gate`) and counted in the console.
  - **Send window**: signal companies are kept for Wednesday and Thursday (`BEST_DAYS = (2, 3)`, was Tuesday and
    Wednesday) and the send worker opens at 08:00 UK time, spreading each mailbox's day over 08:00-12:00 (Belkins 2025,
    7.5M emails: Wed/Thu 0.48%, 08:00-12:00 0.54%).
  - **Copy guard**: `check_outreach_copy(kind, subject, body) -> list[Problem]`, used when scripts and challenger
    subjects are saved and for AI-written copy: first emails 50-125 words, follow-ups at most 90, reading age 12 or
    under, no prices (£/$/€, price, per month, cost with a figure), guarantees, refunds or money back, no calls,
    meetings, Zoom, demos or Calendly, no "free" in a subject (the body may offer the free check), links only to
    https://xevrion.co.uk and https://api.xevrion.co.uk (ops.xevrion.co.uk now redirects, so it is refused in new
    copy). Saved scripts that fail are never deleted: their automation and scheduled batches pause with the reasons,
    shown on the brief in the console, and a start is refused until the script is fixed.
  - **Console**: Outreach has a "Sending guards" card (kit and chart style): spam signals (proxy), bounce rate,
    unsubscribes and paused mailboxes for 7 days, a bullet chart of each mailbox's spam signals against its pause line,

    7-day spark and its pause with the reason and "Lift pause"; then recent pauses, first emails held by the company

- **Customer engine 4b: verified, size-targeted companies from Companies House** (`companies_house.py`, `ch_sourcing.py`).
  - **The register client.** Async httpx with HTTP basic auth from `COMPANIES_HOUSE_API_KEY` (read at run time, never
    logged): company profile, officers, persons with significant control and advanced search. One process-wide token
    bucket of 500 requests per 5 minutes (Companies House allows 600 and blocks the rest of the window after a 429); a
    429 stops every request until `X-Ratelimit-Reset` (else 5 minutes); 5xx and network errors retried 3 times with
    jittered exponential back-off; 10-second time limits. Profiles, officers and PSCs are cached 30 days in `ch_cache`
    by company number. A daily budget (`COMPANIES_HOUSE_DAILY_BUDGET`, default 6,000 requests, counted per UK day in
    `ch_budget`) caps the calls. Structured logs: `companies_house_request`, `_rate_limited`, `_server_error`,
    `_verified`, `_rejected`, `_check_deferred`. Without a key the client reports "not configured" and everything degrades.
  - **Verification**: `verify_company(site_text, site_domain, claimed_name) -> Verification` reads UK company numbers
    only next to the disclosure words the 2015 Trading Disclosures Regulations require (company number / no, registered
    in England, Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland, registration number, reg no, LLP): 8 digits, or SC / NI / OC / SO /
    NC / R0 and 6 digits (7 digits straight after the words are zero-padded). Phone, VAT, charity, FCA and licence
    numbers are refused by their context. A number is accepted only for an active company (not dissolved, in
    liquidation, administration or being struck off) of an allowed type (ltd, plc, llp, companies limited by
    guarantee, private unlimited, Scottish partnership) whose registered name is on the site or matches its name or
    domain (token overlap, 0.6). The prospect gets `company_verified`, `company_number`, `company_name`, `company_type`,
    `sic_codes`, `incorporated_on`, `accounts_type`, `size_band` (micro-entity: micro; small, total-exemption,
    abridged, audit-exempt subsidiary: small; medium; full and group: medium_or_large; dormant or none: unknown),
    `active_directors` and `owner_led` (1-4 active directors, one of them a 25%+ PSC, names normalised). Without a key,
    website evidence only: the number beside the disclosure words AND the registered name with its suffix, matching the
    site: `company_verified: "website_only"`, shown as lower confidence. `public_web.inspect_site` now keeps the
    disclosed numbers and registration lines it reads (`company_numbers`, `registration`); search-found research is
    verified on the way in (`enrich`, in the nightly fill and research jobs).
  - **The send gate is stricter** (`outreach_guards.may_email_company`): with a key only `company_verified is True`
    passes; without one `"website_only"` passes; a bare Ltd / Limited / LLP / PLC word never does now; `size_band`
    micro is always refused (Xevrion's cheapest plan is GBP 295 a month) and unknown passes only for a verified
    company incorporated more than two years ago. Contacts prepared before 4b get the website-only check of their
    stored words at send time; scheduling (`outreach_fill.candidates`) uses the same gate, so held companies no longer
    take capacity.
  - **Companies House first sourcing, all trades** (loop `ch_sourcing`, weekdays from 18:30 UK time; Run now,
    `POST /v1/operator/outreach-sourcing/run`): advanced search for active private limited companies and LLPs
    incorporated two or more years ago, by SIC section, every section except O, T and U, skipping companies whose
    only codes are 64209, 99999, 74990 or 82990; per-code page cursors. Profiles drop micro-entities and unknown
    sizes. The website is found without the 20-a-day search allowance: up to six domains from the registered name

    three hours or 6,000 companies. Search-found research without Companies House verification is re-checked (150 a

## 1.7.0-live63 — learning and memory: fitted models instead of fixed rules

- **Your customers: a fitted BG/NBD model** (`customer_model.py`; Fader, Hardie and Lee, Marketing Science 2005).
  - **Fitting.** Maximum likelihood, fitted to each organisation's own invoice history: Nelder-Mead in log space from three starts, with a 0.001 L2 penalty. Pure Python, run off the event loop.
  - **Outputs.** Each customer's chance of still buying (P(alive)) and the orders expected in the next 90 days.
  - **Check-ins.** For regular customers (3 or more invoices), a check-in is suggested once P(alive) is below 50%, instead of the fixed 1.5 times the usual gap.
  - **When it applies.** Only with 20 or more customers, 8 or more of them repeat buyers, and a converged fit. Otherwise the plain rules apply and the page says which method is in use.
  - **Validation.**
    - On simulated customers it recovers the parameters: r and alpha within a few percent, and the dropout mean within 0.04.
    - Out of sample, fitted on 52 weeks, its 13-week forecast was within 6% of what happened.
    - The tests hold it to 15%.
- **Customer Care learns from the team.**
  - Every reply sent (as written or edited) is kept as an example.
  - A new reply is written with the organisation's most similar past replies beside it, by TF-IDF cosine similarity between the emails. The model is told to match their tone and length, but facts still come only from the saved facts.
  - Examples only fill what is left of the model's 16,000-byte input limit.
  - The learning is measured, not assumed: replies sent without changes this month against last, and how much of an edited reply was kept (difflib ratio). This is shown on the Customer Care page.
- **Improvements learn across organisations.**
  - For each improvement: how many organisations kept it (applied and not undone) against how many turned it down.
  - Scored as a Beta(1, 1) posterior mean with a 95% Wilson range. It orders improvements within a kind.
  - The page shows "Kept by N% of the M organisations that decided on it" once 20 have decided.
  - A "safe" improvement stops being applied by itself once people clearly undo it: the top of its range under 30% after 30 decisions.
